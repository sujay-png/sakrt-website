In the sprawling fields of India, where agriculture forms the backbone of millions of livelihoods, nurturing healthy crops starts beneath the surface… with the roots. For farmers in states like Haryana, Gujarat, and Tamil Nadu, ensuring robust root development is key to thriving harvests, especially in challenging climates.

SAKRT’s biodegradable mulch films offer a eco-friendly solution that not only supports root growth but also aligns with sustainable farming practices. Crafted from plant-based materials, these mulch films create optimal soil conditions, fostering stronger root systems while protecting the environment.

Understanding Mulch Films and Their Agricultural Role

Mulching in agriculture involves covering the soil with a protective layer to optimize growing conditions. In India, where farmers contend with intense heat, unpredictable monsoons, and nutrient-depleted soils, mulch films are a vital tool.

Traditionally, plastic mulch films have been used to retain moisture, control weeds, and regulate soil temperature. However, their environmental drawbacks such as microplastic pollution, have spurred a shift toward biodegradable alternatives. SAKRT’s mulch film rolls, made from compostable vegetable matter, decompose naturally, enriching the soil while supporting plant health.

These lightweight films, typically available in rolls, are easy to install across fields of vegetables, fruits, or ornamental plants. By creating a stable microclimate around the roots, they promote healthier plants and higher yields.

For instance, a farmer in Maharashtra growing onions or a horticulturist in Karnataka cultivating roses can rely on these films to enhance root systems while reducing environmental harm.

The Science of Biodegradable Mulch and Root Growth

How Biodegradable Mulch Affects Root Growth:

Roots are the foundation of plant health, anchoring crops and absorbing water and nutrients. SAKRT’s biodegradable mulch films play a pivotal role in root system development by creating an ideal soil environment.

The films block sunlight, preventing weed growth that competes with crops for resources. This weed suppression ensures that roots, such as those of potatoes or carrots, have unimpeded access to nutrients in the soil, leading to deeper and more extensive root networks.

The mulch films also regulate soil temperature, maintaining a consistent range of 20–30°C, which is optimal for root growth in crops like tomatoes or brinjal. In cooler regions like Himachal Pradesh, the films insulate the soil, keeping roots warm during chilly nights. Conversely, in arid areas like Rajasthan, they prevent overheating, protecting delicate root systems from stress. This temperature moderation encourages roots to grow deeper, accessing water and nutrients from lower soil layers, which is critical during dry spells.

Another key factor is moisture retention. By reducing evaporation, SAKRT’s mulch films ensure that soil remains moist, even in water-scarce regions. For example, a farmer in Andhra Pradesh growing chillies can reduce irrigation frequency by up to 25%, as the films lock in moisture, allowing roots to absorb water steadily. This consistent hydration promotes vigorous root development, resulting in stronger, healthier plants.

Benefits of Biodegradable Mulch Film in Agriculture:

The benefits of biodegradable mulch film in agriculture extend beyond root health. Unlike plastic mulch, which fragments into harmful microplastics, SAKRT’s films break down into organic matter depending on soil conditions. This decomposition process enhances soil structure by adding organic carbon, improving aeration and water-holding capacity. For organic farmers in Punjab or Kerala, this means richer, healthier soil that supports long-term crop productivity.

The effect of mulch films on root system development also translates to tangible yield improvements. Studies show that crops grown with mulch films can see yield increases of 15–30%, depending on the crop and region. For instance, a farmer in Uttar Pradesh using mulch film rolls for sugarcane might harvest an additional 5–10 tonnes per hectare due to stronger root systems and better nutrient uptake. The films’ ability to suppress weeds also reduces labor costs, saving farmers in labor-intensive regions like Bihar valuable time and resources.

Comparison Between Biodegradable and Plastic Mulch:

When comparing biodegradable and plastic mulch, the differences are stark. Plastic mulch, while effective at weed control and moisture retention, often compacts the soil and restricts root expansion over time. Its removal is labor-intensive, and leftover fragments can hinder root growth in subsequent seasons.

In contrast, SAKRT’s biodegradable mulch films decompose naturally, leaving no residue to obstruct roots. This makes them ideal for crop rotation systems common in India, where fields alternate between pulses, cereals, and vegetables.

Additionally, plastic mulch contributes to a growing plastic waste problem, with an estimated 9.46 million tonnes generated annually. Biodegradable mulch films, however, align with India’s sustainability goals, reducing pollution in rural as well as urban ecosystems. For farmers in West Bengal or Odisha, this means cleaner soil and water, which directly benefits root health and crop quality.

Practical Applications in Indian Agriculture

SAKRT’s mulch film rolls are versatile, catering to diverse agricultural and horticultural needs across India. In vegetable farming, they are ideal for crops like cabbage, cauliflower, and capsicum, where strong root systems are essential for high yields. For example, a farmer in Nashik using a 1-meter-wide mulch film roll can protect tomato roots from weeds and drought, boosting output for local markets.

In horticulture, the films support flowering plants like jasmine or marigold, widely grown in South India for cultural and commercial purposes. Their lightweight design, often 25–50 microns thick, makes them easy to lay across raised beds or nursery plots.

Landscaping applications are also gaining traction in urban centers like Delhi and Bengaluru, where biodegradable mulch enhances soil structure in public gardens and private estates.

The role of mulch film in improving soil structure is particularly valuable in degraded soils, common in regions like Vidarbha or Bundelkhand. By preventing erosion and adding organic matter upon decomposition, these films help restore soil fertility, supporting root development for crops like millets or pulses.

Aligning with India’s Sustainable Farming Goals

India’s agricultural sector is at a turning point, with initiatives like the National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA) promoting eco-friendly practices. SAKRT’s biodegradable mulch films align with these goals by reducing reliance on chemical inputs and plastic waste. For instance, a cooperative in Gujarat using these films for groundnut cultivation can market their produce as sustainably grown, appealing to eco-conscious consumers in urban markets like Mumbai or Ahmedabad.

The films also support India’s organic farming movement, backed by schemes like the Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana. By fostering healthier root systems and soil, they enable farmers to transition to chemical-free practices, enhancing both environmental and economic outcomes. SAKRT’s commitment to certified compostable materials ensures that farmers receive high-quality products that meet global standards, building trust across the supply chain.
