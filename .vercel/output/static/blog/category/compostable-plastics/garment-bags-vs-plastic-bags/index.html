Compostable Garment Bags vs Plastic Bags

In today’s fast-changing fashion retail industry, garment bags play a vital role in protecting clothes from dust, moisture, and damage during storage and shipping. However, choosing between traditional plastic bags and newer compostable options has become increasingly important. As environmental awareness grows, many fashion brands are switching to compostable garment bags to reduce their ecological footprint. Unlike plastic bags that take hundreds of years to decompose and often end up in landfills, compostable bags naturally break down without harming the planet.

This shift aligns with broader sustainability trends seen in other single-use products like biodegradable bowls, which are made to decompose rather than persist indefinitely. Plastic waste continues to rise sharply, reaching 460 million tonnes in 2019 alone. Garment bags significantly contribute to this problem, particularly in retail, where millions are discarded each year. By adopting compostable alternatives, businesses can lower their waste output while retaining the same functionality and protection. For instance, plastic waste made up 12% of urban trash in 2018, compared to just 8% in earlier years.

This blog explores the growing debate between compostable and plastic garment bags, examining their benefits, environmental impact, and the data behind the global shift toward sustainability. Beyond garment bags, the rise of compostable tableware and biodegradable bowls shows how eco-friendly innovation is reshaping multiple industries.

The Rise of Compostable Garment Bags in Sustainable Fashion

Compostable garment bags are made from natural materials like polylactic acid (PLA) or cornstarch. These materials allow the bags to break down in industrial composting facilities within a few months. They are made to meet standards like ASTM D6400, which ensures they decompose safely without leaving harmful leftovers. In contrast, regular plastic garment bags are made from polyethylene, a type of plastic that does not break down easily and can stay in the environment for many years.

The increasing popularity of compostable garment bags is because more people are looking for eco-friendly packaging choices. Many stores now provide compostable bags that offer the same protection for clothes as traditional ones. Plus, these bags help reduce microplastic pollution, which often happens when regular plastics start to wear away.

Cloth Packing Bags: Integrating Compostability for Everyday Use

Cloth packing bags are a good reusable choice, but if you need something for one-time use, compostable garment bags are a better option. They feel like regular plastic but are made from plant-based materials, which makes them more friendly for the environment. For dress packing bags, being compostable means they won’t stick around as waste for a long time, even if they’re used only briefly. Numbers show just how serious the waste problem is.

In 1960, the world only made 0.5 million tonnes of plastic, which was less than 0.5% of all waste. By 2000, plastic production had risen to 176 million tonnes, or about 8% of total waste. By 2018, that share had gone up to 12%, which is a 140% increase in just 18 years. If garment bags used 25% compostable options instead of regular plastic, it could help save up to 5 to 7 million tonnes of waste from landfills each year, according to estimates from the fashion industry.

Environmental Impact: Plastic Garment Bags vs. Compostable Alternatives

Plastic garment bags release harmful chemicals as they wear out, with 8 to 14 million tonnes ending up in oceans each year. In contrast, compostable garment bags are broken down by microbes into water and carbon dioxide, leaving behind nutrient-rich compost. This process occurs in controlled environments, making these bags a smarter, eco-friendly choice for stores. Data shows that in 1990, plastic waste was about 156 million tonnes (7% of global waste), rising to 353 million tonnes (12%) by 2019—a 126% increase in volume and a 71% rise in percentage, proving the severity of the issue. Similarly, switching to compostable materials for disposable bowls can reduce food service waste at a comparable rate.

Durability and Performance Comparison
Compostable garment bags match traditional plastic in strength, with tests showing they resist tearing and puncturing effectively. They can carry up to 10 kilograms of weight without damage, making them a dependable option for clothing transport.

Carbon Footprint Calculations
Producing one kilogram of plastic garment bags emits 2–3 kilograms of CO₂, while compostable alternatives generate only 0.5–1 kilogram. Transitioning one billion garment bags from plastic to compostable materials could cut annual CO₂ emissions by 1.5–2 million tonnes—equivalent to removing roughly 400,000 vehicles from the road.

Bags for Retail Shops: Practical Adoption of Compostable Options

Plastic garment bags release harmful chemicals as they wear out, with around 8 to 14 million tonnes ending up in oceans each year. In contrast, compostable garment bags naturally break down into water and carbon dioxide through microbes, leaving nutrient-rich compost. This process happens in controlled environments, making them a smarter, eco-friendly choice for stores. Data shows plastic waste rose sharply—from 156 million tonnes in 1990 (7% of global waste)—highlighting the urgent need for alternatives.

Cloth carry bags also benefit from compostable materials, providing convenience without environmental harm. Unlike plastic, these garment bags don’t form microplastics, which make up 24% of beach litter in some regions. If 500 billion plastic bags are used yearly, with 10% being garment bags, switching to compostable options could eliminate 50 billion persistent plastic items—reducing plastic bag waste in retail from 15% to under 5%.

Dress packing bags require visibility and breathability, achieved through advanced polymer technology in compostable garment bags. These prevent yellowing and mildew—issues common with plastic. Similarly, biodegradable bowls used at events now feature heat resistance through eco-friendly material science innovations.

Compostable garment bags also meet long-term storage needs for cloth coverings. Museums and boutiques rely on them to protect textiles, as decomposition begins only in composting conditions. Though initially 20–30% costlier, landfill savings and stronger brand reputation offset expenses. Over five years, waste management savings can reach 15% per 1,000 units. With over 90 countries banning single-use plastics, the shift to compostable options is accelerating. By 2030, 40% of all packaging, including carry bags, is expected to be compostable.

Conclusion

Garment bags may seem like a small issue, but they highlight a much bigger environmental challenge. In 1990, plastic waste made up 7% of the total, rising to 12% by 2019—a 71% increase. This shows the clear need to switch to compostable garment bags. Along with other eco-friendly options like biodegradable bowls and compostable tableware, these alternatives offer real solutions for positive change. When stores choose compostable materials over plastic, they not only comply with regulations but also support a circular economy. The data proves that reducing waste and environmental harm is achievable. Taking action now with compostable garment bags can lead to a cleaner, greener future for the fashion industry and the planet.
