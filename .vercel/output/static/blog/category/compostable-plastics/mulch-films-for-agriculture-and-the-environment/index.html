The Role of Mulch Films in Modern Agriculture

Mulching, a time-honored practice in agriculture, involves covering the soil around plants to create optimal growing conditions. In India, where monsoons and intense heat can challenge farmers, mulching is a lifeline for maintaining soil moisture and temperature.

Traditionally, plastic mulch films have been used to suppress weeds, retain water, and regulate soil conditions. However, these non-biodegradable synthetic materials often leave behind microplastics, polluting the soil and harming ecosystems.

SAKRT’s compostable mulch films address this issue by offering a sustainable alternative that delivers the same agricultural benefits without the environmental toll.

Made from compostable vegetable matter, these mulch films is lightweight, easy-to-install rolls, they cater to the needs of small-scale vegetable gardeners in states like Punjab and Maharashtra, as well as large-scale horticultural operations in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka.

Whether you’re growing tomatoes in Nashik or spices in Kerala, these films provide a versatile solution for diverse crops and climates. Why Choose Compostable Mulch Films?

One of the standout benefits of compostable mulch film for soil health is its ability to create a balanced growing environment. By covering the soil, the film blocks sunlight, preventing weed growth that competes with crops for nutrients and water. This stronger weed control reduces the need for chemical herbicides, lowering input costs for farmers.

For instance, a farmer cultivating brinjal on a 1-hectare plot could save significantly on labor and weed management by using SAKRT’s mulch films.

Additionally, the films excel at moisture retention, a critical factor in regions like Rajasthan, where water scarcity is a concern. By reducing evaporation, they help maintain consistent soil moisture levels, ensuring crops like okra or chillies thrive even during dry spells. Studies suggest that mulching in agriculture can reduce water usage by up to 30%, making it a boon for farmers reliant on limited irrigation.

The films also regulate soil temperature, keeping roots cool during scorching summers and warm during cooler months, which can extend growing seasons for crops like cabbage or cauliflower.

The result? Improved crop yields and quality. Farmers using agricultural mulch film often report higher outputs, sometimes up to 20% more for vegetables like cucumbers or bell peppers, due to healthier plants and optimized growing conditions.

SAKRT’s mulch film rolls, available in custom widths and lengths, are easy to lay across fields, making them accessible for both small and large farms. Learn more in our blog on environmental significance of compostable products.

Environmental Advantages of Biodegradable Mulch Films

Unlike conventional plastic mulch, which can persist in the soil for decades, SAKRT’s biodegradable mulch films decompose naturally, depending on soil conditions. This decomposition process enriches the soil with organic matter, enhancing its fertility without the need for additional fertilizers.

For organic farmers in Uttarakhand or Andhra Pradesh, this aligns perfectly with their commitment to chemical-free farming.

The environmental advantages of biodegradable mulch films extend beyond the farm. By replacing plastic, these films reduce the risk of microplastic contamination in soil and water bodies, protecting ground water and ecosystems. Moreover, the production of these films involves renewable plant-based materials like cornstarch or sugarcane bagasse, which have a significantly lower carbon footprint compared to petroleum-based plastics.

SAKRT’s commitment to sustainability ensures that their mulch films meet global compostability standards, making them a trusted choice for eco-conscious farmers and horticulturists. By choosing these films, farmers play a role in reducing India’s plastic waste, which is estimated at 9.46 million tonnes annually, and contribute to national goals like the Swachh Bharat Mission.

The films are also customizable to suit specific crops or field sizes.
